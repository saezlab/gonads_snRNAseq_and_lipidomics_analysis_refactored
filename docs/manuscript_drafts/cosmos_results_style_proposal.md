## Single-paragraph proposal

We next asked whether prior molecular knowledge could provide mechanistic explanations for the coordination between gonadal sex-biased transcription and lipid abundance. We assembled an updated mouse prior knowledge network comprising 254,589 signed, directed edges across 52,755 molecular and reaction nodes, integrating signaling and transcriptional regulation with metabolic reactions and lipid–protein interactions. To improve coverage of the measured lipid species, we additionally instantiated reaction hypotheses from mouse-supported templates and compatible lipid structures, retaining these separately from directly curated evidence. We applied the MOON workflow in COSMOS+ using TF activities inferred from hepatocyte Factor 1 RNA weights as upstream inputs and mapped lipid Factor 1 weights as downstream measurements. Before scoring, we compressed nodes with identical signed downstream neighborhoods to reduce repeated propagation of the same information, and iteratively removed transcriptional interactions inconsistent with the RNA signature. Exploring network depth and extraction thresholds identified a selected network with 153 nodes and 388 edges, connecting Ahr, Hnf4a, Ppara and Tbx2 to three diglycerides, DG(20:0_18:1), DG(24:0_18:2) and DG(24:1_18:2). This solution used a six-step pruning radius, four scoring layers and primary/secondary absolute-score cutoffs of 1.85/0.55. A sensitivity analysis allowing more extensive compression recovered the same signed network at these cutoffs. However, Esr1, Nfic and Dbp, candidate regulators highlighted by the correlation analysis, were absent from the solution. All three survived network pruning and compression and received positive lipid-driven MOON scores, which conflicted with their negative RNA-derived Factor 1 activities; MOON therefore excluded them through its upstream-sign consistency rule. Lowering the extraction thresholds could not resolve this discrepancy, and the tested alternative preprocessing settings did not restore sign agreement. These results suggest a limited set of prior-supported regulatory routes to diglyceride abundance, while also exposing gaps between the available interaction knowledge and the measured transcriptional and lipid signatures. The restricted lipid coverage and dependence on species-level reaction hypotheses warrant treating these routes as candidates for experimental testing; exclusion from the model does not establish that Esr1, Nfic or Dbp lack roles in hepatic lipid regulation.
