Results addition

We next asked whether prior molecular knowledge could provide mechanistic explanations for the coordination between gonadal sex-biased transcription and lipid abundance. We assembled an updated mouse prior knowledge network integrating signaling and transcriptional regulation from OmniPath with Mouse-GEM and Rhea metabolic reactions and curated metabolite–protein interactions. This resource comprised 251,998 signed, directed edges; adding 149 explicitly annotated reaction hypotheses connecting measured lipid species expanded the network to 254,589 edges across 52,755 molecular and reaction nodes, including 9,673 gene products. We applied the MOON workflow in COSMOS+ to connect TF activities inferred from hepatocyte Factor 1 RNA weights with downstream lipid Factor 1 weights (Dugourd et al. 2024). After mapping and pruning, the analysis retained 49 upstream TF inputs and 74 lipid observations. We compressed nodes sharing identical signed downstream interactions to limit repeated propagation of the same information, and iteratively removed transcriptional interactions inconsistent with the RNA signature. Exploring network depth and score thresholds identified a network of 153 nodes and 388 edges connecting Ahr, Hnf4a, Ppara and Tbx2 to DG(20:0_18:1), DG(24:0_18:2) and DG(24:1_18:2). The selected configuration balanced network coverage and score thresholds, using a six-step pruning distance, four scoring layers and primary/secondary cutoffs of 1.85/0.55. More extensive compression recovered the same signed network. The proposed routes converged on Smpd1-associated phosphatidylcholine-to-diglyceride reaction hypotheses, whose specificity for these measured lipid species remains to be established. However, Esr1, Nfic and Dbp were absent from the solution despite their strong RNA-derived Factor 1 activities. These TFs survived pruning and compression, but their weak positive lipid-driven MOON scores opposed their negative RNA-derived activities and were therefore excluded by the sign-consistency criterion. This discrepancy may reflect incomplete prior knowledge, limited informative coverage during score propagation, or differences between the RNA and lipid signatures. Thus, causal modelling identified a restricted set of candidate regulatory routes to diglyceride abundance while revealing limitations of prior-guided inference; the absence of expected regulators does not exclude their involvement in hepatic lipid homeostasis.

Methods addition

Prior knowledge-guided modelling of hepatocyte TF–lipid relationships

An updated mouse prior knowledge network (PKN) was assembled to connect signaling, transcriptional regulation, metabolism and metabolite-dependent regulation of protein activity. Signed, directed mouse signaling and CollecTRI transcriptional interactions were retrieved from OmniPath on 8 September 2026 (Türei et al. 2021; Müller-Dott et al. 2023). Metabolic knowledge was obtained from Mouse-GEM (commit fc769ac1da421c4e4e9e1cf2a9aec3fe4f02ef6a), Rhea release 142 and SwissLipids mouse enzyme annotations (Wang et al. 2021). Native mouse metabolite–protein actions were obtained from Guide to Pharmacology release 2026.2 and screened for explicit regulatory signs and chemical-identity consistency. Mouse UniProt identifiers were mapped to primary gene symbols; proteins without an unambiguous single-gene assignment retained accession-based identifiers. Chemical identity and lipid hierarchy were audited using ChEBI release 255 and the SwissLipids snapshot retrieved on 8 September 2026. These sources were assembled through the resource-specific builders in scripts/pkn/ and assemble_mouse_pkn.py; source URLs, versions, checksums, exclusions and evidence records are retained in results/causal_modelling/cosmos_mouse_pkn/ and results/causal_modelling/cosmos_pkn_audit/.

Reactions were represented by explicit catalyst/reaction/direction instances, with positive catalyst-to-reaction, substrate-to-reaction and reaction-to-product connections. Full participants, stoichiometry, compartments, gene–protein–reaction logic and references were retained in the master reaction records. This signed projection represents permissible biochemical connections and does not enforce flux balance, joint substrate availability or changes in metabolite concentration. Unsigned or undirected protein interactions, self-interactions, opposing signs for the same node pair, unverified catalysts and unsupported reaction directions were excluded from the analysis PKN. Unresolved transport compartments and reactions requiring an AND-complex approximation were also omitted. Cached STITCH/BRENDA records and uncertain-direction alternatives were retained only in a separate exploratory resource. Direct metabolite regulatory edges describe protein activity; direct lipid-to-transcript regulation was not established. The curated foundation contained 251,998 edges and 52,157 nodes.

To extend coverage at the measured lipid resolution, eight directional Rhea templates with mouse catalyst support were instantiated between measured diacyl lipids with matching reported acyl-chain compositions, using lipid definitions checked against LINEX2 commit 04e0099655a6b7a8cb57aaeca7b1301b3524079d (Rose et al. 2023). Sum-composition-only annotations, ether and oxidized lipids, sphingolipids and transformations requiring coupled changes in multiple lipid substrates were excluded from this extension. Unresolved positional information was retained as unresolved. The resulting 149 reaction hypotheses involved 33 mouse catalysts, connected 149 measured features and added 2,591 graph edges. Their exact species specificity was explicitly classified as hypothetical, and no observed abundance or correlation sign was used to generate prior edges. The analysis PKN, results/causal_modelling/cosmos_mouse_pkn/pkn_lipid_rules.rds, contained 254,589 edges and 52,755 nodes: 9,673 gene-product nodes, 13 additional protein nodes, 9,116 metabolite nodes and 33,953 catalyst/reaction/direction instances. These instance counts do not represent independent biochemical reactions. Rule generation, lipid mapping and export validation are implemented in scripts/pkn/build_lipid_rules.py, map_lipids.py, audit_mouse_pkn.py and validate_export.R.

Inputs were extracted from the existing MOFA Factor 1 in results/mofa/main_model_RNA_lipid_weight.RData and verified against results/mofa/RNA/mofa_res_12factor.hdf5, without refitting the model. The factor was oriented so that positive sample coordinates were associated with testes; the same orientation was applied to RNA weights, TF activities and lipid weights. All 1,254 lipid feature weights were considered, independently of the 64-lipid subset used for the sparse correlation network. TF activities were inferred from the 20,123 hepatocyte RNA weights using decoupleR ULM with at least 20 measured targets, retaining the original CollecTRI regulons with Ar and Esr1 targets replaced by the study's curated hormone-target sets (support/TF_regulons.RData and support/Gonadal_Hormone_Genes.csv). The 367 reconstructed TF scores matched the saved activities. Upstream inputs required an absolute TF activity greater than 2 and membership in the PKN. Downstream inputs were unscaled lipid Factor 1 weights mapped either to explicit measured-species rule nodes or to unambiguous chemical identities at the reported structural resolution. This yielded 155 mapped observations: 149 rule-associated nodes and six exact ChEBI nodes. Rule association could represent either a substrate or product role, so product-side status was audited separately: 128 initial mapped nodes had incoming reaction-to-product edges, and all 74 lipid observations retained by directed topology pruning were product nodes. Ambiguous and hierarchy-only mappings were excluded; no accepted observation required compartment duplication. Hepatocyte RNA weights were used for transcriptional coherence, rather than as protein-activity measurements or an expression-presence filter. Input extraction, model auditing and mapping are implemented in scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/prepare_factor1_inputs.R, scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/audit_factor1_model.py and scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/map_factor1_inputs.R, with the resulting vectors and mapping decisions saved in miscellaneous/older_results/causal_modelling/cosmos_factor1_pilot/inputs/factor1_inputs.rds. Product-side coverage is audited by scripts/pkn/audit_measured_lipid_reaction_targets.py in results/causal_modelling/lipid_reaction_target_mapping_audit/.

Network inference used the MOON workflow in cosmosR 1.21.2 (commit b2ed195738479c4ad204cace37fd11ae2fd02bb8) and decoupleR 2.17.0 (Dugourd et al. 2024; Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2022). Measured lipids were treated as terminal observations. Directed breadth-first reachability searches retained nodes reachable from at least one upstream TF and capable of reaching at least one lipid observation within the specified pruning distance; pruning and input membership were iterated until stable. In the selected six-step topology, this retained 49 TF inputs, 74 lipid observations and 44,127 edges. Nodes with identical signed child sets were compressed using a safeguarded wrapper around compress_same_children(). Input and TF-regulon identities were protected, including their complete duplicate groups, and the original-to-compressed graph projection was validated. Compression merged 2,720 original nodes into 378 groups, producing a 36,569-edge scoring graph. MOON propagated lipid information upstream through iterative ULM fits with minsize = 1, using the preceding layer as the regression background and retaining each node's first scoring layer. After each scoring pass, the package's filter_incohrent_TF_target() helper removed regulon-supported TF–target interactions inconsistent with the inferred TF score and target RNA weight. Scoring and filtering were repeated until no edges were removed, with a maximum of ten passes. The selected four-layer run converged in three passes. The selected fitting sequence is implemented in scripts/moon/lib/workflow.R, called by scripts/moon/run_factor1_tf_to_lipid.R, with compression and extraction safeguards in scripts/moon/moon_safe_helpers.R.

Parameter sensitivity separated the preprocessing distance from the number of scoring layers, allowing the effect of propagation depth to be examined within the same initially prepared topology. The compressed review examined scoring depths 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 12 at pruning distance 6, and additional distance-4, -5, -7 and -12 topologies at scoring depth 8. Further sensitivities tested initial RNA-coherence filtering and stronger compression at distance 6 with four or eight scoring layers. Stronger compression protected only measured inputs, decompressed scores for original-identifier RNA coherence and recompressed the graph after edge deletion. Initial RNA filtering applied the supplied TF activity signs uniformly to all input TFs, restricting deletions to exact signed interactions supported by both transcriptional evidence and the original input regulon. The expanded dual-threshold search evaluated 2,376 parameter pairs on four unique baseline compressed score states and 5,187 pairs on eight additional compressed states; baseline depths 7, 8, 10 and 12 had identical final states. Primary thresholds ranged from 0.5 to 3.0 and secondary thresholds from 0 to 2.0 in increments of 0.1, with secondary no greater than primary. Local refinement used increments of 0.025 within ±0.1 of threshold/coverage Pareto candidates and near-cap candidates containing 500–999 edges. Scoring sensitivities and extraction searches are implemented in scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/run_factor1_compressed_sensitivities.R, scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/run_factor1_inputs_only_compression.R and scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/search_compressed_threshold_review.R.

Solution extraction used the guarded dual-threshold implementation moon_extract_paths() in scripts/moon/moon_safe_helpers.R. Scores were decompressed to original node identifiers, and extraction used only edges whose compressed counterparts survived the final scoring graph. All inferred nodes required an absolute score above the secondary threshold, and each edge required at least one endpoint above the primary threshold. Measured level-0 lipid weights were exempt from both inferred-score selection criteria and could not themselves seed the primary set. Edges were required to satisfy sign(score_source × score_target) = interaction_sign; supplied TFs additionally required agreement between inferred and input activity signs. Only nodes on a directed walk from an eligible TF to a measured lipid were retained. Candidate solutions required convergence, successful graph audits and fewer than 500 edges; multiple-endpoint comparisons additionally required at least two TFs and two lipids. We selected a representative balancing endpoint coverage, score cutoffs and local threshold sensitivity: pruning distance 6, four scoring layers and primary/secondary thresholds 1.85/0.55. This yielded 153 nodes and 388 edges. It was not a unique mathematical optimum: primary thresholds of 1.85 or 1.875 combined with secondary thresholds of 0.55 or 0.575 produced the same graph. Shortest retained TF-to-lipid routes contained 7–9 edges; neither the scoring depth nor the preprocessing distance was imposed as a path-length bound on the extracted graph. These analyses and checks are recorded in miscellaneous/older_results/causal_modelling/cosmos_factor1_compressed_review/threshold_search/ and scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/audit_compressed_candidate_neighborhood.R.

Expected-regulator retention was audited through input selection, pruning, compression, scoring and final sign filtering. Esr1, Nfic and Dbp were present in the scoring graph but had positive inferred scores of 0.13180, 0.71859 and 0.34516, respectively, opposing their negative supplied TF activities. Diagnostic recomputation without the final upstream-sign constraint exposed these omitted scores but was not used for solution extraction. Target support and regression backgrounds at each TF’s first scoring layer were reconstructed, and alternative topology, compression and RNA-coherence settings were examined. The three TFs retained sign conflicts throughout the tested settings. This audit is implemented in scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/audit_factor1_focus_tf_retention.R, scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/validate_factor1_compressed_tf_sensitivities.R and scripts/moon/archive/2026-09/export_compressed_score_retention.R. Exact network membership, score identity, edge signs, thresholds and TF-to-lipid connectivity were independently checked, and original identifiers, reaction evidence and readable labels were exported with scripts/moon/ancillary/export_factor1_network.py. The selected SIF, node attributes, GraphML and reaction/path evidence are stored in miscellaneous/older_results/causal_modelling/cosmos_factor1_compressed_review/threshold_search/C03/. The selected model is reproduced with Rscript scripts/moon/run_factor1_tf_to_lipid.R, which refits compressed MOON from the archived mapped inputs and PKN, regenerates TF-retention diagnostics and exports, and compares the scores and network with the frozen selected result. The verified refit and audit records are in results/causal_modelling/factor1_tf_to_lipid/; a new --output directory is required for subsequent runs. Network scores and threshold choices were treated as exploratory measures of prior-knowledge consistency, without calibrated significance claims, resampling-based uncertainty estimates or independent validation of the inferred mechanisms.
