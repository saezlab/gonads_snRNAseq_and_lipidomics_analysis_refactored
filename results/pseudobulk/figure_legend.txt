Figure X, Panels B,C — Transcriptional variation by chromosomal and gonadal sex

B,C. For each liver cell type, pseudo-bulk gene expression profiles were summarised by principal component analysis (PCA). The first principal component (PC1) captures the single axis of greatest transcriptional variation across samples; samples with similar expression programmes cluster together, while samples with divergent programmes are spread apart. Ridgeline plots (top) show the density of samples along this axis, coloured by chromosomal sex (B; XX in red, XY in blue) or gonadal sex (C; ovaries in red, testes in blue). Separation of the two coloured distributions indicates that sex is a major driver of transcriptional variation in that cell type, whereas overlapping distributions suggest broadly similar expression between groups. Cell types shown: hepatocytes (Hep), endothelial cells (End), immune cells (Imm), stellate cells (Ste), and cholangiocytes (Cho). Volcano plots (bottom) show differential gene expression in hepatocytes for the corresponding contrast (B, XY vs. XX; C, testes vs. ovaries). Each point represents one gene; the x-axis shows log2 fold change and the y-axis shows -log10 adjusted P-value. Genes with significant upregulation are coloured in blue (XY- or testes-biased) or red (XX- or ovaries-biased); non-significant genes are shown in grey. Selected genes are labelled. The chromosomal sex contrast (B) yields few differentially expressed genes, limited to canonical sex-linked loci (e.g. Xist, Kdm5d, Ddx3y), whereas the gonadal contrast (C) reveals thousands of differentially expressed genes, underscoring gonadal hormones as the dominant driver of hepatic sexual dimorphism.
